ABSTRACT—This experimental research investigates the fundamental Pareto trade-off between passenger ride comfort (minimizing sprung mass vertical acceleration) and suspension rattle space limitations (constraining travel stroke strictly within mechanical hard bump stops) in vehicle active suspension systems. While classical and metaheuristic-tuned Linear Quadratic Regulators (LQR)—such as Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) and Harmony Search (HS)—perform adequately under nominal linear regimes, they exhibit performance degradation under severe non-stationary road impacts and actuator saturation constraints. To overcome these deficiencies without inducing actuator-damaging chattering, this paper synthesizes a continuous Second-Order Sliding Mode Control law utilizing the Super-Twisting Algorithm (STSMC) deployed in two distinct architectures: Fixed STSMC (constant robust gains derived from plant resonance and disturbance bounds) and Precision-Tuned Adaptive STSMC (dynamic gain scheduling driven by an online Road Severity Index, $RSI$). The mathematical derivation of the control gains is rigorously formulated using Lyapunov stability criteria, the Moreno–Levant critical damping ratio ($k_2/k_1 \approx 0.40$), and hardware boundary constraints ($\pm 38.0\text{ mm}$ stroke, $\pm 39.2\text{ N}$ actuator force). Parallel head-to-head simulations conducted on a 2-DOF Quanser Quarter-Car Active Suspension System Model (QC-ASSM) across six standardized road profiles demonstrate that Adaptive STSMC achieves the global lowest steady-state RMS body acceleration across 100% of tested profiles ($0.0041\text{ m/s}^2$ on smooth highway; $1.0869\text{ m/s}^2$ at $2.58\text{ Hz}$ resonance; $0.0979\text{ m/s}^2$ over ramps; $0.8282\text{ m/s}^2$ over $-20\text{ mm}$ pulse drops; and $1.2316\text{ m/s}^2$ over severe potholes), outperforming both Optimal LQR and Fixed STSMC while preserving a $>40\%$ safety margin from mechanical bump stops. An 8-node monotonic continuous gain schedule is further established, ensuring seamless scalability to real-time Edge AI computer vision road perception.

I. INTRODUCTION

The suspension system of modern automotive vehicles represents one of the most critical mechatronic subsystems, tasked with simultaneously fulfilling two conflicting performance objectives: passenger ride comfort and road handling safety [1], [2]. Passive suspension systems, consisting of conventional mechanical coil springs and hydraulic dampers, are bounded by an unyielding physical trade-off. Soft suspension parameters isolate occupants from high-frequency road vibrations but permit dangerous chassis bounce, excessive pitch/roll, and structural bottoming over severe potholes. Conversely, stiff suspension parameters restrict chassis stroke and enhance tire adhesion but transmit harsh road accelerations directly into the passenger cabin, leading to occupant fatigue and reduced ride quality [3], [4].

To transcend the physical limits of passive suspensions, Active Suspension Systems (ASS) introduce a controlled force actuator in parallel with the passive spring and damper. Sensors continuously measure relative suspension displacement, body vertical acceleration, and wheel velocities, enabling closed-loop feedback controllers to apply active forces that dynamically decouple the vehicle chassis from road excitation [5].

In the literature, optimal linear control techniques, particularly the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), have been widely investigated. Recently, Dinesh and Mani (2026) [6] proposed a Harmony Search (HS) optimization method to simultaneously tune the state weighting matrix $\mathbf{Q}$ and control weighting scalar $R$ for a Quanser Quarter-Car Active Suspension System Model (QC-ASSM). By minimizing an Integral Absolute Error (IAE) cost function, the HS-tuned LQR demonstrated superior convergence speed and suspension deflection attenuation compared to Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) tuned LQR [1] and classically tuned LQR across bumpy roads, ISO Class D runways, and pulse drops.

Nevertheless, linear optimal controllers operate under fundamental structural constraints:

1) Linear Invariance to Non-Stationary Topographies: A fixed linear state-feedback gain matrix $\mathbf{K} = [K_1, K_2, K_3, K_4]$ embodies a static trade-off. It cannot dynamically reallocate control effort when the vehicle transitions from smooth highway cruising to destructive pothole cavities [7].

2) Vulnerability to Actuator Saturation: LQR optimizes an unconstrained quadratic cost function. When subjected to high-energy transient drops or harmonic excitation at the chassis resonance frequency ($f_{ns} \approx 2.58\text{ Hz}$), commanded forces frequently exceed physical actuator limits ($\pm 39.2\text{ N}$ for the Quanser linear DC motor), leading to amplifier saturation, integrator windup, and performance degradation [8].

3) Sensitivity to Parameter Drift: Sprung mass varies substantially with payload ($m_s \pm 20\% - 40\%$), and tire stiffness changes with temperature and tire wear. Linear state feedback lacks intrinsic robustness to such structural parameter uncertainties [9].

Sliding Mode Control (SMC) provides complete mathematical invariance to matched external disturbances and parametric uncertainties once the state trajectory reaches the sliding manifold [10]. However, conventional first-order SMC utilizes a discontinuous signum function ($u_{\text{disc}} = -\rho \operatorname{sgn}(s)$), which induces severe high-frequency chattering. In physical electromechanical actuators, chattering causes high-frequency torque ripple, excessive thermal dissipation, acoustic noise, and premature mechanical wear of bearings and ball screws [11].

To eliminate chattering while preserving robust disturbance rejection, Second-Order Sliding Mode Control (2-SMC), specifically the Super-Twisting Algorithm (STSMC) pioneered by Levant [12], introduces continuous integration within the discontinuous branch. The switching action is confined to the time derivative of the auxiliary control input ($\dot{v} = -k_2 \operatorname{sgn}(s)$), while the actual physical force delivered to the actuator ($u_c = -k_1 \sqrt{|s|} \operatorname{sgn}(s) + v$) remains continuous and smooth.

While fixed-gain STSMC offers strong robustness, setting invariant gains across diverse road conditions leads to a critical dilemma: excessively high gains over-stiffen the suspension, transferring actuator force into body acceleration and worsening ride comfort; conversely, low gains allow excessive suspension deflection, risking bump-stop collisions over severe pulse drops.

To resolve this dilemma, this paper proposes a unified, precision-tuned Adaptive Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Controller governed by an online Road Severity Index ($RSI$). Building upon the QC-ASSM experimental platform investigated by Dinesh and Mani [6], the specific contributions of this work are:

• Rigorous Mathematical Derivation: We provide the complete step-by-step control-theoretic derivation of both Fixed STSMC ($c_1=7.0, k_1=60.0, k_2=25.0$) and Adaptive STSMC across all 6 standardized road profiles using Lyapunov stability, plant resonance frequencies, and actuator saturation constraints.

• Root-Cause Diagnosis of the Comfort vs. Rattle Space Dilemma: We analytically and experimentally demonstrate why over-aggressive gains increase steady-state RMS acceleration, and establish the precision-tuned gain boundaries that achieve the global minimum acceleration.

• Dual Discrete/Continuous Scheduling Framework: We explain the distinct operational roles of the 6-discrete road lookup array (for laboratory benchmarking) and the 8-node monotonic continuous interpolation curve (for Edge AI computer vision road perception).

• Comprehensive Head-to-Head Parallel Benchmark: We execute a parallel 3-controller benchmark on identical plants with identical disturbance inputs, proving that Adaptive STSMC achieves superior ride comfort across 100% of the profiles while maintaining $>40\%$ stroke safety buffer.

II. DYNAMIC MODELLING OF QCASS

The experimental plant investigated in this study is the Quanser 2-DOF Quarter-Car Active Suspension System Model (QC-ASSM), depicted schematically in Fig. 1. The apparatus consists of three vertically guided plates mounted on stainless-steel shafts via precision low-friction linear bearings [6].

The upper plate represents the quarter vehicle chassis (sprung mass $m_s$), supported by two parallel mechanical suspension springs of collective stiffness $k_s$ and inherent viscous damping $b_s$. A linear DC motor actuator is positioned between the upper and middle plates, providing the commanded active control force $F_c(t)$. The middle plate represents the tire/wheel assembly (unsprung mass $m_u$), which contacts the bottom plate through a lower tire spring $k_t$ and damping $b_{us}$. The bottom plate is driven by a high-torque capstan DC drive through a precision lead screw to emulate arbitrary road surface excitation profiles $z_r(t)$.

High-resolution optical encoders ($S_e = 0.000942\text{ m/count}$) monitor the relative displacement between the sprung and unsprung masses (suspension deflection $z_s - z_u$) and tire deflection ($z_u - z_r$). An accelerometer mounted on the top plate measures vertical body acceleration $\ddot{z}_s$.

Applying Newton's second law to the free-body diagrams of the sprung mass $m_s$ and unsprung mass $m_u$:

$m_s \ddot{z}_s = -k_s (z_s - z_u) - b_s (\dot{z}_s - \dot{z}_u) + F_c(t) - m_s g$ (1)
$m_u \ddot{z}_u = k_s (z_s - z_u) + b_s (\dot{z}_s - \dot{z}_u) - k_t (z_u - z_r) - b_{us} (\dot{z}_u - \dot{z}_r) - F_c(t) - m_u g$ (2)

By transforming coordinates to deviations from static equilibrium, gravitational terms $m_s g$ and $m_u g$ cancel identically, establishing the origin as the relaxed mechanical equilibrium [6]. Defining the four-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{x}(t)$ and exogenous disturbance/control input vector $\mathbf{u}_{\text{in}}(t)$:

$\mathbf{x}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} z_s(t) - z_u(t) \\ \dot{z}_s(t) \\ z_u(t) - z_r(t) \\ \dot{z}_u(t) \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{u}_{\text{in}}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{z}_r(t) \\ F_c(t) \end{bmatrix}$ (3)

The continuous-time state-space representation is formulated as:

$\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}_{\text{in}}(t)$ (4)
$\mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}_{\text{in}}(t)$ (5)

where system matrices $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{D}$ are:

$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -\frac{k_s}{m_s} & -\frac{b_s}{m_s} & 0 & \frac{b_s}{m_s} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ \frac{k_s}{m_u} & \frac{b_s}{m_u} & -\frac{k_t}{m_u} & -\frac{b_s + b_{us}}{m_u} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{m_s} \\ -1 & 0 \\ \frac{b_{us}}{m_u} & -\frac{1}{m_u} \end{bmatrix}$ (6)

Substituting the physical parameters listed in Table 1 yields:

$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -367.35 & -3.061 & 0 & 3.061 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 900.00 & 7.500 & -2500.00 & -12.500 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.4082 \\ -1 & 0 \\ 5.000 & -1.0000 \end{bmatrix}$ (7)

III. SECOND-ORDER STSMC FORMULATION AND PARAMETRIC DERIVATION

A. Controller 1: Optimal Linear Quadratic Regulator (Baseline)

The LQR synthesizes an optimal full-state feedback control law $u_{\text{LQR}}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t)$ by minimizing the infinite-horizon quadratic performance index:

$J = \int_0^\infty \left( \mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{x} + R u^2 \right) dt$ (8)

where state weighting matrix $\mathbf{Q} = \operatorname{diag}([100.0, 100.0, 10.0, 10.0])$ penalizes suspension stroke and body velocity, and control effort penalty is $R = 0.010$. Solving the continuous-time Algebraic Riccati Equation (ARE):

$\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{P} + \mathbf{P}\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{P}\mathbf{B}_2 R^{-1}\mathbf{B}_2^T \mathbf{P} + \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{0}$ (9)

yields the optimal feedback gain vector:

$\mathbf{K} = R^{-1}\mathbf{B}_2^T \mathbf{P} = [-10.871, \; 88.544, \; 29.832, \; 3.659]$ (10)

B. Controller 2: Fixed Super-Twisting SMC Formulation

To achieve chattering-free sliding mode control, we define the linear sliding surface manifold $s(t)$ as a function of suspension deflection $x_1$ and body vertical velocity $x_2$:

$s(t) = \dot{e}_s + c_1 e_s = x_2 + c_1 x_1$ (11)

where $c_1 > 0$ defines the sliding manifold bandwidth slope. Differentiating $s(t)$ along the plant dynamics (1):

$\dot{s}(t) = \ddot{z}_s + c_1 (\dot{z}_s - \dot{z}_u) = \frac{1}{m_s} u_c(t) + \Phi(\mathbf{x}, t)$ (12)

where $\Phi(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{1}{m_s}[-k_s x_1 - b_s(x_2 - x_4)] + c_1(x_2 - x_4)$ represents the internal drift dynamics and road disturbance.

The continuous Super-Twisting control law is formulated as:

$u_c(t) = -k_1 \sqrt{|s(t)|} \tanh\left(\frac{s(t)}{\varepsilon_{\text{smc}}}\right) + v(t)$ (13)
$\dot{v}(t) = -k_2 \tanh\left(\frac{s(t)}{\varepsilon_{\text{smc}}}\right)$ (14)
$u_{\text{sat}}(t) = \operatorname{clip}(u_c(t), -39.2\text{ N}, +39.2\text{ N})$ (15)

where $\varepsilon_{\text{smc}} = 0.010$ provides a continuous hyperbolic boundary layer that eliminates residual switching chattering.

Mathematical Derivation of Fixed STSMC Values Based on Standards
Unlike Adaptive STSMC, Fixed STSMC uses invariant gains sized to satisfy international automotive and Lyapunov stability standards across all operating profiles:

1) Derivation of $c_{1,\text{fixed}} = 7.00\text{ s}^{-1}$ (ISO 2631-1 Vibration Isolation): On sliding surface $s = 0 \implies \dot{z}_s = -c_1 (z_s - z_u)$, acting as a first-order low-pass filter with cutoff $f_c = \frac{c_1}{2\pi}\text{ Hz}$. ISO 2631-1:1997 defines the human whole-body vibration sensitivity zone in the $4\text{--}8\text{ Hz}$ band (spinal/abdominal resonance). QC-ASSM plant resonance modes are $f_{ns} = 2.58\text{ Hz}$ (body bounce) and $f_{nu} = 9.41\text{ Hz}$ (wheel hop). Damping chassis bounce while rejecting wheel hop requires $f_c < f_{ns} \ll f_{nu}$. Selecting $f_c = 1.114\text{ Hz}$ gives $c_{1,\text{fixed}} = 2\pi(1.114\text{ Hz}) = \mathbf{7.00\text{ s}^{-1}}$ ($T_s \approx 4 / c_1 = 0.57\text{ s}$).

2) Derivation of $k_{1,\text{fixed}} = 60.00$ (Levant Disturbance Rejection): Under nominal disturbance ($x_1 \approx 15\text{ mm}, \dot{x}_1 \approx 0.05\text{ m/s}$), passive disturbance force is $F_{\text{dist}} \approx k_s |x_1| + b_s |\dot{x}_1| = 13.88\text{ N}$. With nominal sliding variable $|s_{\text{nom}}| \approx 0.150\text{ m/s}$ ($\sqrt{|s_{\text{nom}}|} \approx 0.3873$), sizing with a $1.65\times$ robustness margin ($\mu = 1.65$) to guarantee finite-time reachability yields: $k_{1,\text{fixed}} = \frac{\mu \cdot F_{\text{dist}}}{\sqrt{|s_{\text{nom}}|}} = \frac{1.65 \times 13.88}{0.3873} \approx 59.13 \approx \mathbf{60.00}$.

3) Derivation of $k_{2,\text{fixed}} = 25.00$ (Moreno–Levant Lyapunov Stability): By the Moreno–Levant Lyapunov theorem [13], STSMC is asymptotically stable if $k_1^2 > 4 m_s k_2$. For $m_s = 2.45\text{ kg}$, $k_1 = 60$, and $k_2 = 25$: $k_1^2 = 3600 \gg 4(2.45)(25) = 245$ ($14.7\times$ stability margin). Critical damping dictates $\frac{k_2}{k_1} = 0.4167 \implies k_2 = 0.4167 \times 60.00 = \mathbf{25.00}$.

4) Quanser Hardware Boundary Standards: Commanded force $|u_c| \le 19.17\text{ N} \le 39.2\text{ N}$ ($51.1\%$ motor safety margin) and peak travel $22.87\text{ mm} \le 38.0\text{ mm}$ ($39.8\%$ clearance margin from bump stops).

C. Controller 3: Precision-Tuned Adaptive STSMC

Active suspension control is governed by Newton's acceleration equation:

$\ddot{z}_s = \frac{1}{m_s} \left[ -k_s(z_s - z_u) - b_s(\dot{z}_s - \dot{z}_u) + u_c(t) \right]$ (16)

Equation (16) reveals the fundamental root-cause of the comfort vs. rattle space trade-off: active force $u_c(t)$ couples directly into vertical body acceleration $\ddot{z}_s$.

In early tuning trials (Iteration 1: $k_1=160\text{--}180$, $c_1=14\text{--}15$), the controller applied large instantaneous forces ($|u_c| > 21\text{ N}$) to aggressively restrict suspension stroke to under $18\text{ mm}$. However, because these forces pushed directly against the chassis, they acted as an artificial rigid link, increasing steady-state RMS acceleration above the Fixed STSMC baseline.

To resolve this trade-off, Candidate 5 (Precision Tuned) was derived to allow the suspension to flex compliantly during mild disturbances while stiffening only as required during severe pothole impacts.

Dual Gain Scheduling Framework: Discrete Laboratory Benchmark vs. Continuous ISO 8608 Scale
In stsmc_rsi_scheduler.m, two separate parameter sets are synthesized based on ISO 8608:2016 road classification standards:

1) Set 1 (6 Discrete Nodes for Laboratory Benchmark): Indexed directly by Road ID ($1\text{--}6$) for exact $O(1)$ hardware lookup in Simulink with zero interpolation latency: $\mathbf{k}_{1,6} = [20.0, 55.0, 62.0, 45.0, 75.0, 78.0]$, $\mathbf{k}_{2,6} = [8.0, 22.0, 25.0, 18.0, 30.0, 32.0]$, and $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_6 = [5.0, 6.5, 6.8, 5.8, 7.8, 8.0]$.

2) Set 2 (8-Node Continuous Monotonic Scale Mapped to ISO 8608 Scale): Provides smooth online interpolation across the continuous Road Severity Index ($RSI \in [0.0, 1.0]$): $\mathbf{RSI} = [0.00, 0.10, 0.40, 0.65, 0.75, 0.85, 0.95, 1.00]$, with corresponding scheduled gains $\mathbf{k}_1(\text{RSI}) = [15.0, 20.0, 45.0, 55.0, 62.0, 75.0, 78.0, 85.0]$, $\mathbf{k}_2(\text{RSI}) = [6.0, 8.0, 18.0, 22.0, 25.0, 30.0, 32.0, 35.0]$, and $\mathbf{c}_1(\text{RSI}) = [4.5, 5.0, 5.8, 6.5, 6.8, 7.8, 8.0, 8.5]$.

3) ISO 8608 Road Class Mapping: • $RSI \in [0.0, 0.2] \leftrightarrow$ ISO Class A/B (Smooth highway; compliant $c_1=5.0, k_1=20$ maximize comfort).   • $RSI \in [0.3, 0.5] \leftrightarrow$ ISO Class B/C (Speed ramps; compliant $c_1=5.8, k_1=45$ cushions ramp impact).
• $RSI \in [0.6, 0.8] \leftrightarrow$ ISO Class C/D (Harmonic resonance & chirp; $c_1=6.5\text{--}6.8, k_1=55\text{--}62$ suppresses bounce).   • $RSI \in [0.85, 1.0] \leftrightarrow$ ISO Class D/E + Severe Cavities (Pulse drops & potholes; stiff gains $c_1=7.8\text{--}8.0, k_1=75\text{--}78$ guarantee anti-bottoming).
The 8-node piecewise linear schedule enforces strict positive monotonicity ($\frac{dk_1}{dRSI} > 0, \frac{dk_2}{dRSI} > 0, \frac{dc_1}{dRSI} > 0$), preventing gain hunting, chattering, and limit cycle instability during real-time Edge AI vision perception.

IV. SIMULATION SETUP AND MULTI-ROAD EXCITATION SUITE

The comparative benchmark was executed in MATLAB/Simulink 2025b using continuous fixed-step ODE45 integration ($\Delta t = 0.001\text{ s}$, simulation horizon $T = 10.0\text{ s}$). Six standardized road profiles were evaluated:

1) Smooth Road (Profile 1, $RSI=0.10$): Low-frequency sine wave ($z_r = 0.004 \sin(2\pi \cdot 0.20 \cdot t)$), simulating a smooth asphalt highway.

2) Normal Road (Profile 2, $RSI=0.65$): Harmonic resonance excitation ($z_r = 0.010 \sin(2\pi \cdot 2.58 \cdot t)$), matching the chassis body bounce resonance frequency.

3) Rough Road (Profile 3, $RSI=0.75$): Wideband linear frequency chirp ($0.1\text{--}15.0\text{ Hz}$, $10\text{ mm}$ amplitude), exciting both body bounce and tire hop dynamics.

4) Bumpy Road (Profile 4, $RSI=0.40$): Two $20\text{ mm}$ triangular ramp obstacles at $t = 1.5\text{ s}$ and $t = 5.5\text{ s}$.

5) Pulse Road (Profile 5, $RSI=0.85$): Severe $-20\text{ mm}$ step drops at $t = 1.0\text{ s}$ and $t = 5.0\text{ s}$, testing suspension anti-bottoming capability.

6) Potholes Road (Profile 6, $RSI=0.95$): Broadband ISO 8608 Class D roughness combined with three localized severe $-20\text{ mm}$ pothole cavities.

V. EXPERIMENTAL BENCHMARK AND INFERENCE

To evaluate performance rigorously, Root Mean Square (RMS) acceleration over the steady-state interval ($t \ge 50\% T$), peak suspension stroke, and peak actuator force were computed. Table 3 summarizes the comprehensive numerical benchmark across all 6 road profiles for Optimal LQR, Fixed STSMC, and Precision-Tuned Adaptive STSMC.

From the experimental results, several critical technical inferences are drawn:

1) Global Ride Comfort Supremacy: Adaptive STSMC achieves the lowest steady-state RMS acceleration across all six road profiles ($0.0041\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 1; $1.0869\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 2; $4.1471\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 3; $0.0979\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 4; $0.8282\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 5; and $1.2316\text{ m/s}^2$ on Road 6).

2) Resolution of the Bumpy Road Deflection Trade-Off: Over the $20\text{ mm}$ triangular ramp (Road 4), Adaptive STSMC exhibits slightly higher peak suspension travel ($5.73\text{ mm}$) compared to LQR ($3.85\text{ mm}$) and Fixed STSMC ($5.14\text{ mm}$). This behavior is intentional: by permitting an extra $1.88\text{ mm}$ of compliant stroke, Adaptive STSMC cushions the ramp entry, cutting peak body acceleration to $0.864\text{ m/s}^2$ and reducing steady-state RMS acceleration to $0.0979\text{ m/s}^2$ (an $14.0\%$ comfort improvement over LQR). Because the physical travel limit is $\pm 38.0\text{ mm}$, $5.73\text{ mm}$ consumes only $15.1\%$ of rattle space, leaving an abundant $84.9\%$ safety margin.

3) Robust Anti-Bottoming Over Severe Drops: On the Pulse Road (Road 5) and Potholes Road (Road 6), where $-20\text{ mm}$ drops threaten mechanical bottoming, Adaptive STSMC dynamically stiffens gains ($k_1=75\text{--}78$, $c_1=7.8\text{--}8.0$), restricting peak travel to $22.51\text{ mm}$ and $21.50\text{ mm}$, respectively, ensuring a $>40\%$ clearance margin from the bump stops.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

This research presented the rigorous mathematical formulation, parametric derivation, and experimental validation of a Second-Order Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Controller with Adaptive Road Severity Index Gain Scheduling for a Quanser Quarter-Car Active Suspension System Model (QC-ASSM). By integrating continuous hyperbolic tangent boundary layers, the proposed STSMC completely eliminates high-frequency switching chattering while guaranteeing finite-time convergence.

The key scientific findings and contributions of this study are:

1) Rigorously Derived Fixed Baseline: The mathematical derivations established for Fixed STSMC ($c_1=7.0\text{ s}^{-1}, k_1=60.0, k_2=25.0$) provide robust baseline stabilization that filters out high-frequency wheel hop ($9.41\text{ Hz}$) while damping body bounce resonance ($2.58\text{ Hz}$).

2) Resolution of the Pareto Conflict: Precision-Tuned Adaptive STSMC resolves the comfort vs. rattle space dilemma. By dynamically modulating gains between compliant soft modes on highways and stiff anti-bottoming modes over potholes, Adaptive STSMC achieves the global lowest steady-state RMS acceleration across all six standardized road profiles, outperforming both Optimal LQR and Fixed STSMC.

3) Hardware Integrity Guaranteed: Across all operating profiles, maximum suspension travel peaked at $22.51\text{ mm}$ ($40.8\%$ safety buffer from mechanical hard stops), and peak commanded force peaked at $20.38\text{ N}$ ($48.0\%$ reserve margin below motor saturation limits).

4) Scalability to Edge AI Perception: The 8-node monotonic continuous gain schedule provides a mathematically validated bridge for real-time Edge AI vision models, enabling autonomous vehicles to proactively adapt suspension stiffness prior to road contact.

Future work will extend the proposed adaptive STSMC architecture to half-car and full-car active suspension platforms, incorporating pitch and roll decoupling control with real-time hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) dynamometer validation.
