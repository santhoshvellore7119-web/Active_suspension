1. Executive Summary & Engineering Motivation

Automotive suspension design has always been governed by an unyielding mechanical conflict: passenger ride comfort demands a soft, compliant connection between the vehicle body (sprung mass) and the axle to isolate high-frequency road vibrations, while handling and stability require a stiff suspension that firmly keeps the tire on the tarmac and tightly limits chassis heave, roll, and pitch.

On a conventional passive suspension, a fixed spring stiffness ($k_s$) and hydraulic damping ($b_s$) represent an immutable compromise. If the vehicle strikes a severe pothole, a soft passive damper bottoms out against the mechanical travel stops ($\pm 38.0\text{ mm}$ on the Quanser platform), transmitting harsh, structural impact forces into the chassis. Conversely, on high-frequency washboard roads, a stiff damper transmits high vertical accelerations directly into the passenger cabin.

Key Engineering Solution
To resolve this fundamental tripartite trade-off without compromise, we engineered a complete active suspension architecture comprising: (1) A standardized 6-Road Profile Generator encapsulating speed chirps, highway heave, discrete potholes, ramp obstacles, ISO Class D roughness, and harmonic body resonance; (2) A 2nd-Order Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Controller (STSMC) that provides robust, finite-time disturbance rejection with continuous, chattering-free actuation; and (3) An adaptive Road Surface Index (RSI) gain scheduler that scales control stiffness dynamically according to real-time road severity.

2. Quanser 2-DOF Quarter-Car Dynamics & Physical Constraints

The physical system represents one wheel assembly and one-quarter of the vehicle body. The state vector $x(t) = [x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4]^T$ is formulated around physically measurable quantities:

  • $x_1(t) = z_s(t) - z_{us}(t)$: Suspension Travel / Relative Stroke (m)
  • $x_2(t) = \dot{z}_s(t)$: Sprung Mass (Vehicle Body) Vertical Velocity (m/s)
  • $x_3(t) = z_{us}(t) - z_r(t)$: Dynamic Tire Deflection (m)
  • $x_4(t) = \dot{z}_{us}(t)$: Unsprung Mass (Wheel) Vertical Velocity (m/s)

Applying Newton's second law to both masses yields the state-space formulation:

$$\dot{x}(t) = A x(t) + B_{\text{ctrl}} F_c(t) + B_{\text{dist}} \dot{z}_r(t)$$ $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -\frac{k_s}{m_s} & -\frac{b_s}{m_s} & 0 & \frac{b_s}{m_s} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ \frac{k_s}{m_u} & \frac{b_s}{m_u} & -\frac{k_t}{m_u} & -\frac{b_s + b_{us}}{m_u} \end{bmatrix}, \quad B_{\text{ctrl}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \frac{1}{m_s} \\ 0 \\ -\frac{1}{m_u} \end{bmatrix}, \quad B_{\text{dist}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -1 \\ \frac{b_{us}}{m_u} \end{bmatrix}$$

3. The 6 Standardized Road Profiles: Physics, Formulation & Dynamic Behavior

Real-world terrain excitations exhibit diverse spectral, temporal, and amplitude characteristics. Testing active suspensions solely against a simple sine wave fails to evaluate critical transient behaviors such as deadbeat settling, rebound ringing, and resonance avoidance. We engineered 6 standardized automotive road profiles, all cleanly encapsulated inside the Road Surface Generator subsystem.

Figure 2: Internal architecture of the Road Surface Generator subsystem, routing 6 distinct road profiles through a 6-input Multiport Switch controlled by the number-entry constant block.

Profile 1: Dynamic Frequency-Sweep Chirp ($0.1 - 10.0\text{ Hz}$)

Scenario & Physics: A vehicle accelerating continuously away from a standstill over an undulating road, sweeping from crawl speeds to highway velocities. The chirp excitation probes the entire frequency bandwidth of the suspension, passing through body hop resonance ($2.58\text{ Hz}$) and approaching wheel hop ($10.5\text{ Hz}$) to verify phase stability.

$$z_r(t) = A_{\text{chirp}} \sin\left(2\pi\left(f_0 t + \frac{1}{2} k_{\text{chirp}} t^2\right)\right), \quad A_{\text{chirp}} = 5.0\text{ mm}, \quad f_0 = 0.1\text{ Hz}, \quad f_1 = 10.0\text{ Hz}$$
Figure 3: System response under Profile 1 (Chirp Sweep). The STSMC controller maintains stable, bounded stroke and smooth acceleration across the entire $0.1 - 10\text{ Hz}$ bandwidth.

Profile 2: Smooth Undulating Highway ($0.20\text{ Hz}$, $4.0\text{ mm}$)

Scenario & Physics: High-speed cruising on freshly paved motorway tarmac with long-wavelength thermal expansion swells. In this regime, passengers are exceptionally sensitive to micro-vibrations in the $0.1 - 0.5\text{ Hz}$ nausea-inducing range. The controller must remain exceptionally compliant, providing "cloud-like" ride comfort without wasting control effort.

$$z_r(t) = A_{\text{smooth}} \sin(2\pi f_{\text{smooth}} t), \quad A_{\text{smooth}} = 4.0\text{ mm}, \quad f_{\text{smooth}} = 0.20\text{ Hz}$$
Figure 4: System response under Profile 2 (Smooth Highway). Acceleration is suppressed to negligible levels with minimal actuator force expenditure ($< 0.8\text{ N}$).

Profile 3: Severe Discrete Potholes ($-20.0\text{ mm}$ Drop Cavities)

Scenario & Physics: Urban driving over broken asphalt with sharp-edged sunken cavities. When a tire drops abruptly into a $20\text{ mm}$ void, an unassisted suspension crashes into the negative travel limits and exhibits violent underdamped ringing. The STSMC provides immediate counter-thrust to support the body, achieving deadbeat settling along the sliding surface in $< 0.12\text{ s}$.

$$z_r(t) = \begin{cases} -20.0\text{ mm}, & \text{if } t \in [k T_p + 0.2, \; k T_p + 0.5)\text{ s}, \quad k \in \mathbb{N} \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
Figure 5: System response under Profile 3 (Severe Potholes). Notice the rapid deadbeat recovery without secondary bounce or travel violation.

Profile 4: Triangular Ramp Obstacle & Speed Bump ($+20.0\text{ mm}$ Wedge)

Scenario & Physics: Traversing elevated speed tables or expansion joints with a gradual incline followed by an abrupt vertical drop-off. The suspension must compress smoothly up the incline without nose-pitching, then instantaneously expand to absorb the step drop without bottoming the rebound rail.

$$z_r(t) = \begin{cases} 20.0 \cdot \frac{t - 0.5}{1.0}\text{ mm}, & 0.5 \le t < 1.5\text{ s} \quad (\text{Gradual Incline}) \\ 20.0 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{t - 1.5}{0.02}\right)\text{ mm}, & 1.5 \le t < 1.52\text{ s} \quad (\text{Abrupt Drop-off}) \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
Figure 6: System response under Profile 4 (Triangular Ramp). The suspension tracks the ramp slope while maintaining chassis levelness and damping the drop-off edge.

Profile 5: Stochastic Rough Terrain + Embedded Shock Spikes (ISO Class D)

Scenario & Physics: Traveling at $90\text{ km/h}$ over an unpaved gravel road containing random spectral chatter superimposed with discrete stone impact shocks. Tests the controller's ability to filter broadband random noise while simultaneously rejecting high-amplitude transient shock pulses.

$$z_r(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} A_i \sin(\omega_i t + \phi_i) + \sum_{j=1}^{N} A_{\text{shock}} \cdot e^{-\frac{(t - t_j)^2}{2\sigma^2}}, \quad A_{\text{broadband}} = 15.0\text{ mm}, \quad A_{\text{shock}} = 25.0\text{ mm}$$
Figure 7: System response under Profile 5 (Stochastic Rough Terrain + Shock Spikes). The STSMC successfully isolates high-frequency body vibrations while safely absorbing isolated $25\text{ mm}$ shocks.

Profile 6: Harmonic Corrugated Resonance Road ($2.58\text{ Hz}$ Body Hop Mode)

Scenario & Physics: A washboard dirt road with repeating ridges tuned exactly to the quarter-car fundamental body hop frequency ($f_{n,1} \approx 2.58\text{ Hz}$).

Resonance Safety Alert
Without active intervention, passive suspension resonates violently at $2.58\text{ Hz}$, reaching a destructive peak stroke of $74.85\text{ mm}$—shattering the $\pm 38.0\text{ mm}$ guide rail stops. The STSMC provides powerful out-of-phase anti-resonance damping, clamping peak stroke to $10.18\text{ mm}$ ($73.2\%$ safety margin) and eliminating chassis bottoming.
$$z_r(t) = A_{\text{res}} \sin(2\pi f_{\text{res}} t), \quad A_{\text{res}} = 10.0\text{ mm}, \quad f_{\text{res}} = 2.58\text{ Hz}$$
Figure 8: System response under Profile 6 (Resonance Road). The active controller completely tames the $2.58\text{ Hz}$ body mode, preventing destructive rail bottoming.

4. Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Control (STSMC) Design

Classical 1st-order sliding mode control uses discontinuous switching $u = -K \operatorname{sgn}(s)$, which induces severe chattering—high-frequency switching that damages gears and overheats motors. Arie Levant's Super-Twisting algorithm resolves this by driving both $s$ and $\dot{s}$ to zero in finite time while producing an inherently continuous control signal.

Figure 9: Internal Simulink implementation of the STSMC_Controller subsystem: State demuxing, sliding surface generation, non-linear proportional branch, continuous integrator, and saturation limiter.

4.1 Sliding Surface Formulation

The sliding surface $s(t)$ blends body velocity damping ($\dot{z}_s = x_2$) with suspension stroke centering ($z_s - z_{us} = x_1$):

$$s(t) = \dot{e}(t) + c_1 e(t) = x_2(t) + c_1 x_1(t)$$

When constrained on $s(t) = 0$, body velocity decays exponentially: $\dot{z}_s = -c_1 (z_s - z_{us})$, pulling the vehicle body smoothly to equilibrium.

4.2 Continuous Control Law with Hyperbolic Tangent Boundary Layer

To ensure 100% smooth actuation and protect the Quanser motor drive from current spikes, we approximated the discontinuous signum function with a continuous hyperbolic tangent boundary layer ($\epsilon_{bl} = 0.010$):

$$u_{\text{stsmc}}(t) = -k_1 \sqrt{|s(t)|} \tanh\left(\frac{s(t)}{\epsilon_{bl}}\right) + w_{\text{int}}(t)$$ $$\dot{w}_{\text{int}}(t) = -k_2 \tanh\left(\frac{s(t)}{\epsilon_{bl}}\right)$$ $$F_c(t) = \operatorname{sat}\left(u_{\text{stsmc}}(t), \;-39.2\text{ N}, \;+39.2\text{ N}\right)$$

4.3 Lyapunov Finite-Time Stability

Using the quadratic Lyapunov candidate $V = \xi^T P \xi$ with state $\xi = [|s|^{1/2} \operatorname{sgn}(s), \; w_{\text{int}}]^T$:

$$P = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 4k_2 + k_1^2 & -k_1 \\ -k_1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \operatorname{det}(P) = 2k_2 + \frac{1}{4}k_1^2 > 0$$

Since $P$ is strictly positive-definite for all $k_1, k_2 > 0$, taking the time derivative yields $\dot{V} \le -\gamma V^{1/2}$ ($\gamma > 0$), strictly guaranteeing finite-time convergence to the sliding manifold $s = 0$.

7. Operational Quick-Start Guide

The entire simulation suite can be executed either via a single MATLAB command or interactively through the Simulink canvas: 

% 1. Switch to project folder:
cd('C:\Users\Santhosh\Documents\antigravity\zealous-borg');

% 2. Run any of the 6 road profiles in one command:
simulate_road(3);   % Simulates Road 3 (Severe Potholes) with full metrics
simulate_road(6);   % Simulates Road 6 (Harmonic Resonance)

% 3. Open the graphical Simulink model:
open_system('Master_Active_Suspension_Simulation');

Inside Simulink, open Road Surface Generator, double-click Enter Road Number (1 to 6), enter any integer from 1 to 6, and press Ctrl + T to simulate.
